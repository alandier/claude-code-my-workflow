\section{Robustness of the Puzzle}
% ===================================================================

\begin{frame}{The Correlation Is Not Mechanical (Lagged ROA Test)}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\centering
\includegraphics[height=0.52\textheight]{scatter_lag_roa_vs_pe.pdf}

\vspace{-0.05cm}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Concern}: NI is in both ROA (numerator) and P/E (denominator) --- a temporary earnings shock mechanically raises ROA and lowers P/E.

\textbf{Test}: replace ROA$_t$ with ROA$_{t-1}$. Last year's NI is not in this year's P/E.

\textbf{Result}: correlation barely changes ($-$0.41 $\to$ $-$0.37). Country ROA effects are extremely persistent ($r = 0.99$), so the link reflects \emph{permanent} profitability differences.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Not Industry Composition Either}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Specification} & \textbf{ROA vs P/E} & \textbf{Note} \\
\midrule
Raw country averages & $-$0.02 & No controls \\
Industry$\times$year demeaned & $+$0.11 & Industry removed, no other controls \\
\addlinespace
Regression \textbf{without} industry FE & $-$0.46 & Size + leverage + earn.\ growth \\
Regression \textbf{with} industry FE & $-$0.41 & Our main specification \\
\addlinespace
Using lagged ROA$_{t-1}$ & $-$0.37 & Breaks NI$\to$P/E mechanical link \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Industry FE slightly \emph{reduce} the correlation ($-$0.46 $\to$ $-$0.41) --- industry composition is not the driver
\item The negative link emerges after controlling for firm size (see next slide)
\item Raw correlation is near zero because size heterogeneity masks the relationship
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{It Is a Simpson's Paradox: Size Controls Reveal the Correlation}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}llcc@{}}
\toprule
& & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{$r$(ROA, P/E)}} \\
\cmidrule(lr){3-4}
\textbf{P/E controls} & \textbf{ROA controls} & \textbf{Local} & \textbf{USD} \\
\midrule
None & None & $-$0.02 & $-$0.02 \\
$+$ size & None & --- & $-$0.14 \\
None & $+$ size & --- & $-$0.15 \\
\addlinespace
\textbf{$+$ size} & \textbf{$+$ size} & $\mathbf{-0.51}$ & $\mathbf{-0.64}$ \\
$+$ size $+$ leverage & $+$ size $+$ leverage & --- & $-$0.65 \\
Full spec & $+$ size $+$ industry & $-$0.47 & $-$0.59 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.1cm}
\scriptsize
The negative ROA--P/E correlation is \textbf{zero} without size controls. It appears \emph{only} when both P/E and ROA regressions control for firm size. The effect is \textbf{stronger} with USD-converted size ($r = -0.64$) --- not a currency artifact.

\textbf{Interpretation (Simpson's paradox)}: within each size bucket, high-ROA countries have lower P/E. But across size buckets, the relationship is masked.
\end{frame}

\section{Summary and Next Steps}
% ===================================================================

\begin{frame}{Stylized Facts}
\footnotesize
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \textbf{Country dominates valuation} --- 65\% of $R^2$ for P/E (Shapley). Extends Heston--Rouwenhorst (1994) from returns to levels.

\item \textbf{P/E is cleaner than M/B} --- M/B contaminated by leverage ($+$0.90***) and accounting (Japan: M/B $-$0.35 vs P/E $\approx$ 0).

\item \textbf{Valuation dispersion is diverging} --- P/E dispersion $\times$2.7 despite financial globalization.

\item \textbf{Negative ROA--valuation correlation across countries} ($\rho \approx -0.4$). Within countries, the sign flips positive ($+$0.80). A Simpson's paradox driven by size conditioning.

\item \textbf{National savings rate predicts P/E} ($r = +0.34$, $t = 3.01$). The only significant macro predictor. More savings $\Rightarrow$ more capital chasing equities $\Rightarrow$ higher multiples.

\item \textbf{Standard institutional variables are null} --- La Porta, financial development, GDP per capita: all near zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Research Questions}
\small
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{3pt}
\item \textbf{Why is P/E dispersion diverging?} ($\times$2.7) --- US tech? Post-GFC monetary policy? Capital flows?

\item \textbf{Can the savings channel be formalized?} --- Savings/GNI predicts P/E but is orthogonal to ROA. Can we build a model where savings rates affect discount rates and therefore valuations?

\item \textbf{Do low-valuation countries earn higher returns?} --- Bekaert--Harvey, Fama--French international. If so, valuation gaps reflect risk premia.

\item \textbf{What explains China's P/E premium?} --- Highest P/E despite moderate M/B. Retail investors? Capital controls? Savings rate (46\% of GNI)?

\item \textbf{EV/EBITDA as third metric} --- avoids both the book equity issues (M/B) and the profitable-firms restriction (P/E).
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Related Literature}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Country vs industry effects:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item Heston--Rouwenhorst (1994, \textit{JFE}) --- country dominates returns. We extend to \emph{levels}.
\item Griffin--Karolyi (1998, \textit{JFE}) --- industry effects vary across sectors.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Institutions and investor protection:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item La Porta et al.\ (2002, \textit{JF}) --- investor protection raises Tobin's Q. Our results: weak for P/E.
\item Hail--Leuz (2006, \textit{JAR}) --- institutions reduce cost of equity by up to 200 bps.
\item Stulz (2005, \textit{JF}) --- ``twin agency problems'' prevent equalization.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Savings and asset prices:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item Abel (2003, \textit{Brookings}) --- effect of baby boomers' saving on asset prices.
\item Caballero et al.\ (2008, \textit{JME}) --- global imbalances and asset price inflation.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Regression Specifications}
\small
\textbf{P/E} (profitable firms, $NI > 0$):
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{align*}
\log(P/E)_{i,t} &= \beta_1 \text{Lev} + \beta_2 \log(\text{Assets}) + \beta_3 \Delta E_{t-1} + \gamma_c + \delta_j + \tau_t + \varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{align*}

\vspace{-0.4cm}
\textbf{M/B} (all firms):
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{align*}
\log(M/B)_{i,t} &= \beta_1 \text{ROA} + \beta_2 \text{Lev} + \beta_3 \log(\text{Assets}) + \beta_4 \Delta E_{t-1} + \text{FE} + \varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{align*}

\vspace{-0.4cm}
\textbf{ROA}:
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{align*}
\text{ROA}_{i,t} &= \beta_1 \text{Lev} + \beta_2 \log(\text{Assets}) + \gamma_c + \delta_j + \tau_t + \varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{align*}

\vspace{-0.3cm}
\footnotesize FE = $\gamma_c$ (country) + $\delta_j$ (GICS industry) + $\tau_t$ (year).
SE clustered at country level. Spec 2: $\gamma_{c,t}$ (country$\times$year) replaces $\gamma_c + \tau_t$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Market Cap Construction (Detail)}
\footnotesize
\textbf{US firms}: \texttt{mkvalt} (pre-computed market value in USD millions). Available from 1986.

\vspace{0.1cm}
\textbf{Non-US firms} (Compustat Global): no pre-computed market value.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item Compute: Market Cap = \texttt{cshoi} (shares outstanding) $\times$ \texttt{prccm} (month-end price)
\item \textbf{Pitfall}: firms have multiple securities across exchanges and currencies

\vspace{0.05cm}
\emph{Example --- SAP SE (gvkey=103487) in Dec 2022:}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
iid=02W & EUR & 96.39 $\leftarrow$ \textcolor{accent}{correct} \\
iid=05W & HUF & 38{,}620 $\leftarrow$ \textcolor{alert}{wrong currency!} \\
iid=06W & MXN & 2{,}009 $\leftarrow$ \textcolor{alert}{wrong currency!} \\
\end{tabular}

\item \textbf{Solution}: use \texttt{prirow} field (primary security designation) to select the correct iid
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.05cm}
All data in local currency (millions). Cross-country \emph{levels} absorbed by country FE.
\end{frame}
